THE RECORDING

Ten sows, one working room, nobody watching

Between April 26 and May 20 2026 we ran the sensing rig on ten pre-farrowing sows in a working farrowing room at the Texas A&M swine center, under IACUC oversight, with the center's own staff running the room around us. It recorded through the nights. Everything below came out of that recording.

10pre-farrowing sows, one working farrowing room
500+ hof depth, RGB and IR farrowing video recorded
4detection tasks, all four outputs shown on this page
Apr–May2026 trial window, under IACUC oversight

Two axes, two kinds of evidence

The grayscale wide frames below come from the forward-looking view along the sow's body, which is where posture and respiration live. The color close frames come from the downward view over the landing zone, which is where a newborn appears. Nothing on this page is cropped from a single camera to look like two.

Why the footage looks like this

It is 5 frames per second at 640 by 480, at night, through a barn camera, because that is the hardware a unit has to work on and the light a farrowing room actually has. We have not upscaled it, color graded it or cut around a failure. The overlays are the model's, not a designer's.

DOWNWARD AXIS · NEWBORN DETECTION

A birth, caught at the moment it happens

Twenty one seconds of the landing zone under a sow in labor. For the first thirteen seconds there is nothing to report and the counter in the header reads zero. Then a piglet arrives, the model boxes it, labels it a newborn and the counter turns over. No stockperson was in the room.

This is the event the whole alert chain hangs from. A birth that is seen can be timed, and a birth that is timed is what makes the next one being late mean something.

DOWNWARD AXIS · STILLBORN DISCRIMINATION

Same model, same crate, two different answers

Finding a piglet is the easy half. The half that matters is telling a piglet that needs help from one that is already gone, and that is a question about movement, not appearance. So the model holds a track on each piglet and watches how far it actually travels. Here are both outcomes, from the same barn and the same overlay, side by side. Between the two clips the path length differs by about 3.4 times and the movement figure by about 5 times, on the same camera, the same night and the same model. That gap is the whole basis of the call.

Stillborn precision measured at 96% and newborn precision at 86% on held-out trial video. Newborn precision is the binding limit on the alert chain and we say so on the product page. A flag is a prompt for a stockperson to look, never a diagnosis.

FORWARD AXIS · SOW POSTURE CLASSIFICATION

Six postures, read in the dark

Crushing is a posture problem, so the forward view has one job: name what the sow is doing, all night, without a wearable and without a light on. Below is one real frame per class, straight out of the classifier, with the label and confidence it returned printed in the corner where it wrote them. Pick a posture to see its frame.

These frames are dark because the room was dark. We have left the exposure alone, since how the classifier copes with a real barn at three in the morning is the point of showing it.

Posture classification reached 99.15% on a sow the model had never seen. The 0.85 on one of the six frames above is shown on purpose: the classifier is not uniformly certain, and the sternal classes are where it is least certain, which is exactly where a crushing decision would be made.

FORWARD AXIS · RESPIRATION

Breathing, taken from the video itself

Nothing touches the sow. The forward view watches the rise and fall of her flank, and the pipeline pulls a periodic signal out of that motion and fits a rate to it. This is the output for one 20 second window from the trial, exactly as the script drew it.

Then we checked it against a human counting breaths

A fitted curve is only worth what it agrees with, so we built every plausible way of reading respiration off this barn and scored all of them against a manual breath count. Three sensors, two trackers, both motion axes and two body regions give 18 channels, 100 clips each. The point of running all 18 was to find out which one to put in the product, and the answer was not the obvious one.

2.43 bpm mean absolute error against a manual count, for the winning channel: the depth sensor reading her back

Depth on the back won on both error measures, and it won in a way that matters more than the average. Its ratio of RMSE to MAE is 1.37, the lowest of all 18 channels, where everything else lands between 1.8 and 3.1. A low ratio means the errors it makes are small ones. The other channels are dragged by occasional large misses, which is the failure mode that would put a false distress alert in front of a stockperson at four in the morning.

This is also why the forward head on the product is a stereo depth module rather than a second color camera. We did not pick depth because it sounded better. It came first out of 18.

Every channel was scored on the same detector boxes. RGB, infrared and depth all arrive at 848 by 480, so one set of YOLO boxes transfers across all three streams untouched, which means a difference between sensors is the sensor and not a difference in where we looked.

The plot above is a research output and it looks like one. We would rather show the figure the pipeline produced than a prettier version we drew afterward. On the comparison: n is 100 clips per channel and the reference is a manual re-count, so these are error figures against a human counter rather than against a clinical instrument. Two things are still open and we are not treating them as done: the Bland-Altman agreement analysis is not finished, and recounts on 13 flagged clips are still to be confirmed. Respiration is a welfare and distress signal here, and we make no clinical claim from it.

READ THIS PART TOO

What this proves,
and what it doesn't

Footage is persuasive, which is exactly why it needs a boundary drawn around it. Here is ours.

What it does prove

The perception works on real animals, in a real farrowing room, at night, on barn-grade video, without touching the sow. Births are caught when they happen. A still piglet and a moving one come out on opposite sides of a measurable gap. Posture is named through all six classes. Breathing comes off the video with no contact. None of that is a simulation and none of it is a render.

What it does not prove

This is one trial, on one farm, with ten sows, recorded on the prototype rig rather than the sealed product on the other pages. No producer has bought or run the system yet, so nothing here is a customer result and we do not claim one. Multi-farm validation is planned, not done, and models trained in one barn can be wrong in another. That is why the product is built to prompt a stockperson to look, and never to decide alone.

Trial conducted April 26 to May 20 2026 at the Texas A&M University swine center under IACUC oversight.

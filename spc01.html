THE CRUSHING EVENT

Three seconds, one direction

Crushing kills more piglets than any disease, and it is not random. Most crushing deaths happen during one specific event: the sow rolling from sternal to lateral lying. The roll goes to one side that is knowable in advance, and the window is about three seconds long. An alert cannot cross a farrowing room that fast. An actuator that is already mounted on the crate can.

~30%of preweaning loss is crushing, the single largest cause
80%of crushing deaths happen in the first four days after farrowing
68%happen during the sternal-to-lateral roll
~ 3 sduration of the roll, too fast for any human response

Why alarms are not enough

A posture monitor can flag a sternal-lying sow with piglets in her roll path, but the fastest stockperson is minutes away and the roll takes seconds. Detection without an actuator leaves the last meter of the problem unsolved.

Why a curtain works

The curtain never has to stop a 250 kg sow. It only has to keep 2 kg piglets out of the strip of floor she is about to occupy. That is a light fabric barrier, closed early and lifted as soon as she settles, so nursing access is restored within seconds.

Crushing shares and roll statistics from published preweaning-mortality literature. SPC-01 is a concept industrial design, not production hardware.

THE RULE

Four postures, one rule

Crushing is a one-way transition. On her feet nothing is resolved yet, so both curtains stay down. From sternal lying she can roll to exactly one side without getting up, so only that side stays curtained. Both lateral postures are terminal, no further roll is possible from them. So the rule fits in five lines.

THE MECHANISM

A roller, and nothing else

The whole product is one overhead roller assembly. No posts on the floor, no rails, no moving parts at piglet height.

FABRIC

TPU-coated barrier

A 1.0 m curtain of coated polyester drops to the creep floor on a weighted hem. It stops a piglet, wipes clean under a pressure washer and weighs almost nothing.

DRIVE

Tubular motor, sealed

The motor lives inside the tube, the way awning drives do. Full travel takes about two seconds, quiet enough not to startle the room.

FAIL SAFE

Open by default

Power loss, signal loss or a manual pull on the coral release all end the same way: curtain up, and the crate behaving exactly like a crate without an SPC-01.

THE SYSTEM

Half of a closed loop

SPC-01 is the actuator half of the Smart Swine farrowing system. The eyes belong to SPM-01, the dual-axis monitor already watching the crate from the rear. While the sow is on her feet both strips stay curtained. The moment she settles into sternal lying it knows which side she can roll to, and over the sealed wireless link the safe curtain lifts while the danger side holds.

SPC-01 · Smart Piglet Curtain

Acts. Two independent curtain units close the danger zone SPM-01 has named, and lift when it clears. Each product also operates on its own.

01 · DETECT

SPM-01 reads the sow

The forward depth head classifies standing, sternal and lateral lying continuously, with no wearables and no floor sensors.

⟶

02 · DECIDE

The danger side is named

Sternal lying resolves to a rollable side. That side's nursing strip becomes the danger zone until her posture changes.

⟶

03 · ACT

SPC-01 answers the posture

Standing keeps both curtains down. Sternal lying lifts the safe side and holds the roll path closed. Lateral lying lifts both, and nursing resumes.

INSTALLATION

Two brackets, half a turn each

Every fastener goes to the crate and nothing touches the floor, because the floor is pressure washed and scraped.

SADDLE CLAMP

Hangs on the top rail

A rubber-lined saddle snaps over the crate's top rail and a captive thumb screw closes it. No drilling, no welding, no tools, and removal is just as fast.

END BRACKETS

Grip only what never turns

The two drop arms hold the static motor housing and the idler pin, exactly like a roller blind's end brackets, so the winding fabric is never obstructed.

FITS THE FLEET

Standard NA crates

The bracket span matches the common North American farrowing-crate top rail, and both curtain units install from the aisle in minutes.

DESIGN BRIEF

Designed to be harmless first

A device that acts inside a farrowing crate has to be safe against its own mistakes. Three behaviors are part of the design brief, ahead of any performance figure.

Safety behaviors

The hem carries no cutting edge and the drive is torque-limited: a curtain that meets a piglet on the way down reverses and retries. Both units fail open on any loss of power or signal. The manual release needs one hand and no tools, and always ends with both curtains up.

Honest limits

SPC-01 is a concept industrial design under active development. The mechanism, mounting and materials shown are design proposals, not a production build, and no field performance is claimed for the curtain itself. The posture detection it relies on is the validated SPM-01 pipeline.
